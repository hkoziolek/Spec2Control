The LNG production train starts with feed-gas reception and pretreatment. Pipeline gas enters a slug catcher where level-controlled dump valves isolate liquids, then flows through coalescing inlet separators outfitted with differential-pressure transmitters that alarm at 10 kPa to prompt filter change-out. Meter runs and chromatographs send real-time composition data to the DCS, while booster compressors raise pressure to 6–8 MPa for stable downstream hydraulics. An amine absorber removes H₂S and CO₂ under temperature-controlled lean-amine circulation; a flash tank–lean/rich heat-exchanger pair, reboiler steam flow, and absorber overhead CO₂ analyzer close the acid-gas loop. The sweet gas next passes through molecular-sieve dehydration beds sequenced by ΔP and outlet H₂O ppm alarms, followed by activated-carbon mercury guard beds protected with inlet/outlet Hg analyzers to <0.01 µg Nm⁻³ before entering the cryogenic section.

In the heavy-hydrocarbon and NGL recovery block, the dry gas is cooled in a brazed-aluminum cold box by propane refrigerant to −30 °C, partially condensing C₃+ components. A de-ethanizer and de-propanizer train operating at 1.2–2.4 MPa strips LPG-grade liquids; column pressure and reflux ratios are modulated by on-line RVP, C₂/C₃ ratio, and tray-temperature profiles. An expander–compressor skid then drops the gas stream to −95 °C, boosting liquid yield while regaining shaft power that is recycled to the sales-gas booster. Hydrocarbon liquid levels in the demethanizer reflux drum are maintained with cascade control to the recirculation pump VSDs, preventing methane slip into the condensate product.

The heart of the facility is the liquefaction and refrigerant system. In a dual-mixed-refrigerant (DMR) configuration, a propane pre-cool loop (−35 °C), followed by a mixed-refrigerant loop (−160 °C), duties a vertical aluminium Main Cryogenic Heat Exchanger (MCHE) 1.2 m in diameter and 45 m long. Gas-turbine-driven centrifugal compressors (three casings per loop) run at 5 000–10 000 r min⁻¹, with active-magnetic bearings providing vibration data into a high-speed condition-monitoring system. Discharge pressures, suction temperatures, and MR composition analyzers feed model-predictive control that minimises specific energy, while anti-surge valves act within 150 ms of set-point deviation. End-flash drums at 0.1 MPa drop the liquefied stream to −162 °C, routing boil-off gas (BOG) to low-duty compressors and subcooling any warm return via a BOG recondenser.

Storage and ship-loading complete the cold chain. LNG at −160 °C is pumped via 450-m³ h⁻¹ in-tank pumps into full-containment storage tanks (200 000 m³ each) with outer post-stressed concrete walls and 9 %-Ni steel inner shells. Level radar, tank-top temperature strings, and annular-gap H₂ sensors interface to a SIL-3 tank-overfill prevention system that locks out rundown pumps when 97 % full. BOG generated from diurnal heat-inleak is compressed to 0.6 MPa and either routed back to the MR loop or to a 25 MW gas turbine in the utility plant. Loading pumps transfer product through vacuum-jacketed lines to articulated marine loading arms equipped with hydraulically interlocked emergency-release couplers and custody-transfer Coriolis mass meters capable of 12 000 m³ h⁻¹.

A dedicated utilities and safeguarding layer underpins the process. The plant’s combined-cycle power block delivers 150 MW at 11 kV, with black-start diesels on an automatic transfer switch. Instrument-air compressors, nitrogen generators (PSA and cryogenic), seawater lift pumps, and a closed-loop glycol heating system all report health variables—vibration, temperature, ΔP—to the central asset-management server. Emergency-shutdown (ESD) systems isolate feed gas within 3 s via pneumatic HIPPS valves, while fire-and-gas detectors initiate active deluge and dry-chemical suppression. The distributed control system aggregates 15 000 I/O points across OPC-UA servers, structuring interlocks, permissives, and sequence controls that form the backbone for any advanced automation narrative, from start-up logic through steady-state optimisation and safe plant trip handling.
