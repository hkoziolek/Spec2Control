The process starts with wood handling and chemical pulping. Debarked logs are chipped in a knife-ring chipper and screened to 12–25 mm length before entering a continuous digester operating at 160–175 °C and 700–900 kPa. White liquor flow is ratio-controlled to the dry-chip feed to maintain an effective alkali of 15–18 % and a target Kappa number of 17–22. Chip level, digester temperature, and pressure are cascaded to steam-flow and liquor-recirculation loops; blow-line consistency transmitters feed a model-predictive controller that stabilises pulp quality as the slurry drops to the blow tank at ≈100 °C.

In the washing, screening, and bleaching train, brown stock exits through twin-roll press washers or diffusion washers that reduce residual spent liquor to <5 kg NaOH ∙ t⁻¹ pulp. Multistage knotters, centrifugal cleaners, and pressure screens remove shives and sand, while density and pressure profiles protect slot plates from wear. Bleaching sequences (typically O-D-E-P) run in up-flow towers with retention times of 60–180 min at controlled pH (2.5–11, depending on stage) and 45–80 °C. High-consistency (10–12 %) pulp is refined in double-disc refiners equipped with specific-edge-load control (0.4–1.0 W s mm⁻²) and motor-load feedback, after which the stock is diluted to 3 % consistency in the machine chest.

Approach-flow and forming prepare the fibre slurry for the paper machine. A decoupled fan pump maintains 350–550 kPa head to the headbox, and vacuum deaerators keep dissolved oxygen below 3 mg L⁻¹. Consistency transmitters, basis-weight valves, and a cross-directionally profiled dilution water system trim sheet weight within ±1.5 g m⁻². At the headbox, turbulence generators equalise velocity before the jet lands on a 30–40 m s⁻¹ gap former or fourdrinier wire. Table foils, vacuum boxes (30–60 kPa), and hydrofoils draw out water to attain 18–22 % dryness by the couch roll, with slice-lip actuators and CD sensors closing the loop on sheet thickness and moisture.

The partially dewatered web enters the press and dryer sections. A two-nip shoe-press train builds linear loads up to 1 500 kN m⁻¹, elevating sheet dryness to 42–46 % while press fabric moisture is monitored by microwave sensors. Press section temperatures are limited to 65 °C by closed-loop shower water and fabric conditioning. In the dryer, 80–120 steam-heated cylinders operate at 250–550 kPa internal pressure; hood air is kept at 90–110 °C with 150 m³ s⁻¹ exhaust fans linked to VOC-capture scrubbers. Infra-red moisture scanners and web-tension cells modulate steam pressure, pocket ventilation, and draw control to reach 92–94 % final dryness without sheet breaks.

Finishing and winding complete the product. A soft-nip calender stack running at 25–40 m s⁻¹ and 250–300 kN m⁻¹ nip load smooths the sheet, followed by an optional film-size press or blade-coating station where starch or pigment is metered at 1–4 g m⁻² with LASER-based coat-weight sensors. After a 35 m long after-dryer and web-cooling section, an automated reel builds parent rolls up to 8 m in width under tension-controlled spools. A dual-drum slitter-winder trims and separates customer rolls, which are bar-coded, wrapped, and conveyed to climate-controlled warehouses. All equipment states, interlocks, and alarms integrate into a distributed control system that tracks fibre, energy, and additive balances, providing the backbone for a robust automation narrative across the entire paper-making line.
