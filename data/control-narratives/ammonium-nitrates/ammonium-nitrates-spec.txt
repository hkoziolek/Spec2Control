The production train of the ammonium nitrates plant begins with continuous neutralization, where ammonia gas and 55-70 wt % nitric acid are combined in a titanium-lined, steam-jacketed neutralizer operating just below atmospheric pressure. Ammonia flow is metered through a mass-flow controller and sparged beneath the liquid surface, while acid enters via a coriolis-metered loop with cascade ratio control. Rapid exothermic reaction maintains the liquor at 175-185 °C; temperature is trimmed by variable steam injection and a vent scrubber recovers stripped ammonia. Level, pH, and density transmitters feed a DCS that holds the product at roughly 60 wt % NH₄NO₃ before it overflows to the next stage.

In the evaporation and conditioning section, the neutral liquor is routed to a triple-effect forced-circulation evaporator constructed of 25-22-2 stainless steel. Each calandria operates under progressively deeper vacuum, driving the melt to 99 wt % while minimizing thermal decomposition. Condensate is returned as boiler feedwater, and a barometric condenser vents non-condensables through a scrubber. Key control variables include melt temperature, specific gravity, and vacuum level; high-temperature alarms (≈210 °C) trip steam to protect against runaway. The dense melt is discharged by a molten-salt jacketed pump to the solidification step.

Solidification is achieved either in a prilling tower or, in modern plants, a fluidized-bed granulator. For prilling, melt is sprayed from a rotating bucket 30–40 m above ground; counter-current ambient air cools droplets as they fall, forming nearly spherical prills collected on a vibrating screen deck. In a granulator, the melt is atomized onto recycled seed particles within a fluidized bed while conditioned air (humidity < 15 g H₂O/kg) hardens successive layers. Both configurations employ melt flow control, bed temperature regulation, and exhaust-gas scrubbing to limit ammonium nitrate “fume.” Product leaving this stage meets a target size of 2–4 mm and ≤0.3 wt % moisture.

A cooling and coating circuit follows to arrest caking and thermal cycling. Rotary or belt coolers reduce product temperature below 50 °C using ambient or chilled air, with dew-point monitoring to prevent condensation. An inline weight-belt feeder meters cooled prills into a rotating drum where 0.4–0.6 wt % of an anti-caking/anti-dust agent—often stearate-based—is sprayed on via air-atomizing nozzles. Dust is extracted through baghouse filters equipped with ΔP-controlled pulse cleaning. Real-time particle-size analyzers and moisture probes close the control loop for final product quality assurance.

Finally, coated product is transferred by covered belt to storage silos outfitted with continuous radar level gauges, over-pressure relief, and aeration pads for uniform discharge. A reclaim screw, weighbridge, and pneumatic load-out spouts interface with the shipping area, all sequenced through a PLC interlock matrix that enforces temperature, humidity, and inventory set-points. Utility systems—instrument air, demineralized water, steam, and power—are monitored for redundancy and alarmed to a central control room. Safety instrumented functions include high pH shutdown in the neutralizer, high melt temperature trips in the evaporator, and combustible-dust concentration alarms in the prilling tower exhaust. These layers of control provide the backbone for an automation narrative that assures throughput, product quality, and safe operation across the entire ammonium-nitrate process.
