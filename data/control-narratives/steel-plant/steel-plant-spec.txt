The steel plant is designed to convert iron ore, coal, and fluxes into crude steel through an integrated blast furnace and basic oxygen furnace configuration. Iron ore is agglomerated in a sinter plant and charged to the blast furnace together with coke produced in the on-site coking facility. Hot blast air and injected pulverized coal provide the required reduction conditions, generating molten hot metal and slag. The hot metal is tapped continuously and transferred to the steelmaking stage.

In the steel shop, the hot metal is refined in basic oxygen furnaces (BOF), where oxygen is blown into the molten iron to reduce carbon, sulfur, and other impurities. Alloying additions are introduced to achieve the specified steel grades. Secondary metallurgy processes such as ladle furnace treatment, vacuum degassing, and inclusion modification ensure tight control of composition and quality. Continuous casting machines solidify the liquid steel into slabs, blooms, or billets as semi-finished products.

The downstream rolling mills convert the cast semi-finished products into finished steel products, including hot-rolled coils, plates, bars, and structural profiles. The plant is configured for high throughput and flexible product mix, enabling efficient use of raw materials and energy. Off-gases from the blast furnace, coke oven, and BOF are recovered as by-product fuels to generate steam and electricity, minimizing external energy demand. Dust, slag, and other solid by-products are processed for reuse or sale, ensuring compliance with environmental and economic targets.
