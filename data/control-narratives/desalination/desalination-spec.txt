The control narrative for the Desalination Plant outlines the philosophy and operational logic for the plant’s intake system, which is responsible for abstracting feed water from either seawater or reservoir sources. The intake system consists of dedicated pipelines for both water sources, a sophisticated screening system, air-blast cleaning mechanisms, sampling and shock chlorination systems, sump pumps for flood protection, and four intake pump trains. The operational modes, status, and responses of the intake system are clearly defined, allowing for either reservoir or seawater abstraction depending on plant requirements. Each subsystem—such as screens, pumps, air compressors, and sampling units—has specific status definitions (e.g., AVAILABLE, RUNNING, STOPPED, TRIPPED) and interlocks to ensure coordinated, safe, and reliable operation.

The intake screens play a crucial role in protecting downstream equipment and aquatic fauna by preventing debris and organisms from entering the plant. There are six screens for seawater and four for reservoir water, all of which are passive (no moving parts) and periodically cleaned by scheduled air-blast bursts. The air-blast system uses compressed air provided by two duty compressors and air receivers, delivering controlled bursts to dislodge debris. The cleaning sequence is fully automated and can be adjusted by operators, with safety features and alarms to handle any valve or compressor failures. In the event of pollution incidents, buoy-mounted analyzers monitor water quality parameters, and the plant can execute safe shutdown procedures as necessary.

The intake pumps, equipped with variable frequency drives (VFDs), are managed to deliver the required flow to the plant based on real-time demand and process feedback. Up to four pumps (three duty, one standby) can be operated, with controls to balance load and running hours. Pump operation is closely monitored with permissives and interlocks based on water levels, pump conditions, and downstream system status. Automatic changeover ensures continuous operation in case of pump or fan failure, and leak detection systems are in place to identify and isolate faulty pump trains. The pumps are coordinated with the overall plant master control system, integrating start-up, shutdown, and ramping sequences to maintain stable and efficient operations.

Shock chlorination is implemented as a biofouling control measure, particularly for the seawater intake screens and chambers. Dosing sequences for sodium hypochlorite are automated and adjustable, ensuring periodic disinfection without excessive chemical use. The system includes interlocks and alarms for chemical availability and dosing pump status, with integration to the plant’s dechlorination process to protect downstream equipment and water quality. The intake bypass flow and sampling systems further enhance operational flexibility and monitoring, providing means to flush pipelines and continuously assess water quality using multiple analyzers and alarms tailored to the source water mode.

Flood protection for the intake system is achieved through a combination of level sensors, automatic sump pumps, leak detection, and CCTV monitoring. The sump system automatically cycles pumps based on water accumulation, prioritizing equipment with the least running hours and allowing operator overrides as needed. High-level alarms and prolonged run-time alarms ensure timely operator intervention in case of abnormal water ingress. The narrative concludes with comprehensive equipment, instrument, and valve lists, supporting detailed maintenance and troubleshooting activities, and ensuring that all operational and safety contingencies are addressed for reliable plant performance.
