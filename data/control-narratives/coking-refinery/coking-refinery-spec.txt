The coking refinery is designed to process heavy and sour crude oils with a significant proportion of residue. Crude oil is first routed to the atmospheric distillation unit (ADU), where it is separated into light gases, stabilized naphtha, kerosene, diesel fractions, and atmospheric residue. The atmospheric residue is further treated in the vacuum distillation unit (VDU), producing vacuum gas oil (VGO) and vacuum residue as key feedstocks for downstream conversion.

The vacuum gas oil is directed to the fluid catalytic cracking (FCC) unit to maximize gasoline and LPG production, while also generating light cycle oil for diesel blending. The vacuum residue is thermally cracked in a delayed coking unit operating at high temperature, producing coker naphtha, coker gas oil, and solid petroleum coke. Supporting hydrotreating units upgrade the quality of naphtha, kerosene, diesel, and gas oils by removing sulfur and improving fuel specifications.

The overall configuration achieves deep conversion of crude oil with minimal residual fuel oil output. The targeted product slate emphasizes high-octane gasoline, ultra-low sulfur diesel, and jet fuel, supplemented by LPG and petrochemical feedstocks. Petroleum coke is recovered as a by-product suitable for use in power generation or aluminum smelting, depending on its grade. The refinery is designed to maintain operational flexibility across a wide range of crude oil qualities, ensuring stable production and economic optimization.
